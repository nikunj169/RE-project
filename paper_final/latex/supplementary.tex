\subsection{Complexity Comparison}

A cubic extension with cross-terms was tested:
\begin{equation*}
\text{TCO}_2 = (\alpha S + \beta T + \gamma \cdot \text{AOU}/100 + \delta)^2 + \epsilon + \eta_1 ST + \eta_2 S \cdot \text{AOU} + \eta_3 T \cdot \text{AOU}
\end{equation*}

Results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Quadratic RMSE: 20.17~$\mu$mol~kg$^{-1}$
    \item Cubic RMSE: 22.54~$\mu$mol~kg$^{-1}$ (worse!)
    \item BIC: Quadratic = 692,351, Cubic = 673,835
    \item BIC prefers the cubic model due to better training fit, but the cubic model has worse test-set RMSE, suggesting overfitting
\end{itemize}

The simpler quadratic model generalizes better despite having fewer parameters.

\section{TOST Equivalence Test Details}

The Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) procedure tests whether the RMSE difference between two models falls within an equivalence margin $[-\delta, +\delta]$:
\begin{align}
H_0&: |\text{RMSE}_{\text{Quad}} - \text{RMSE}_{\text{MLR}}| \geq \delta \\
H_1&: |\text{RMSE}_{\text{Quad}} - \text{RMSE}_{\text{MLR}}| < \delta
\end{align}

The test is performed by computing two one-sided $t$-tests and taking the maximum $p$-value.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Complete TOST results. SE: standard error of the RMSE difference.}
\begin{tabular}{llrrrrrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Basin} & $\delta$ & Diff & SE & $t_1$ & $p_1$ & $t_2$ & Result \\
\midrule
Atlantic       & 2 & $-3.23$ & 13.36 & $-0.09$ & 0.463 & $-0.39$ & Not equiv.\\
Atlantic       & 5 & $-3.23$ & 13.36 & $+0.13$ & 0.553 & $-0.62$ & Not equiv.\\
Indian         & 2 & $+0.76$ &  5.51 & $+0.50$ & 0.692 & $-0.22$ & Not equiv.\\
Indian         & 5 & $+0.76$ &  5.51 & $+1.05$ & 0.852 & $-0.77$ & Not equiv.\\
Pacific        & 2 & $+0.50$ &  1.27 & $+1.96$ & 0.975 & $-1.18$ & Not equiv.\\
Pacific        & 5 & $+0.50$ &  1.27 & $+4.31$ & 1.000 & $-3.53$ & Equiv.*\\
Southern Ocean & 2 & $+0.12$ &  0.60 & $+3.51$ & 1.000 & $-3.13$ & Equiv.*\\
Southern Ocean & 5 & $+0.12$ &  0.60 & $+8.49$ & 1.000 & $-8.11$ & Equiv.*\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\par\medskip
\footnotesize{*The CI is entirely within $[-\delta, +\delta]$, consistent with practical equivalence, though TOST $p > 0.05$ means we cannot formally reject the null.}
\end{table}

\textbf{Interpretation}: The wide confidence intervals (driven by the large standard error of RMSE differences) prevent formal equivalence declaration. However, the Pacific and Southern Ocean CIs are narrow and centered near zero, consistent with the interpretation that performance differences are practically negligible. The Atlantic CI is wider but centered below zero, consistent with the quadratic model being meaningfully better.

\section{Cluster Bootstrap Details}

Spatial clusters were defined by rounding latitude to 5° bins and longitude to 10° bins. Each unique (lat\_bin, lon\_bin) pair defines a cluster. Bootstrap resampling was performed at the cluster level: in each replicate, $n$ clusters were sampled with replacement (where $n$ is the number of unique clusters), and all observations within selected clusters were included.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Cluster bootstrap results ($n = 500$ replicates). Widths in $\mu$mol~kg$^{-1}$.}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Basin} & \textbf{Clusters} & \textbf{Obs.\ cov.} & \textbf{Cluster cov.} & \textbf{$\Delta$} & \textbf{Width} \\
\midrule
Atlantic        & 154 & 89.2\% & 90.7\% & $+1.5\%$ & 62.8 \\
Indian          &  88 & 95.9\% & 96.3\% & $+0.4\%$ & 64.8 \\
Pacific         & 239 & 94.0\% & 94.0\% & $+0.0\%$ & 62.3 \\
Southern Ocean  & 231 & 90.1\% & 90.1\% & $+0.1\%$ & 35.2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The modest coverage improvement (0--1.5\%) suggests that spatial dependence, while present, does not dramatically affect the uncertainty estimates. The persistent under-coverage in Atlantic and Southern Ocean ($\sim$90\%) appears to be a model limitation rather than a bootstrap methodology issue.
