# Abstract

Anthropogenic CO₂ (C_ant) in the ocean interior is inferred from repeated measurements of dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) or from transient-tracer age distributions under steady circulation. The two are usually compared only as gridded inventories. Here we apply one within-location estimator to observed DIC and to tracer-based C_ant from TRACE at the same 342,277 GLODAPv2.2023 samples (515 cruises, 1972–2021), conditioning on potential temperature, salinity and apparent oxygen utilisation, by basin and neutral-density layer.

To separate ocean change from slow calibration drift, we add a differential estimator with cruise fixed effects. It is immune to any additive per-cruise offset, including offsets that drift with time, and was validated on synthetic data (bias ≤ 0.002 µmol kg⁻¹ yr⁻¹).

Observed DIC at fixed properties rose by ≈1 µmol kg⁻¹ yr⁻¹ in the lightest layers of every basin, declining with density. In the upper ocean (γⁿ ≤ 27.0 kg m⁻³) it accumulated 16 % [11–21 %] faster than TRACE predicts, and 18 % [11–23 %] faster relative to the deep ocean once all cruise offsets are removed. The excess was similar in all four basins (16–20 %) and robust to every analysis variant. It persisted under TRACE's ventilation-age and transit-time-shape uncertainties, and it was matched at the same samples by two independent eMLR(C*) products (13–20 %). These products are derived from the same DIC data but with a different method.

An Earth system model sampled at the same points provides a test independent of GLODAP DIC. In it, the estimators recover the exact anthropogenic carbon rate (no positive bias), and TRACE falls 12 % short of the model's upper-ocean accumulation.

Replacing TRACE's damped surface-ocean CO₂ boundary condition by the atmospheric record removes about 60 % of the observed excess and most of the model-world shortfall. A remaining 6 % [1–10 %] points to intermediate-water ventilation ages or natural carbon change. The deep Atlantic, by contrast, accumulates 25–35 % less carbon than the steady-state tracer expectation in both observations and eMLR(C*).

These results provide a drift-immune observational constraint on tracer-based C_ant accumulation rates. They also show that static empirical DIC models validated on later years are dominated by this anthropogenic trend.
