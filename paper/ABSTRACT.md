# Abstract

Anthropogenic CO₂ (C_ant) in the ocean interior is inferred either from repeated measurements of dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) or from transient-tracer age distributions under steady circulation, and the two rarely meet at the level of individual samples. We applied one within-location fixed-effects estimator to observed DIC and to tracer-based C_ant from TRACE at the same 342,277 GLODAPv2.2023 samples (515 cruises, 1972–2021). The estimator conditions on potential temperature, salinity and apparent oxygen utilisation and is resolved by basin and neutral-density layer. Observed DIC at fixed properties rose by ≈1.0–1.1 µmol kg⁻¹ yr⁻¹ in the lightest layers of all basins, declining with density to 0.29 µmol kg⁻¹ yr⁻¹ in North Atlantic Deep Water and to near zero in deep Indian Ocean water. In a pre-registered test, the observed-to-tracer rate ratio was statistically consistent with 1 ± 0.15 in 18 of 20 upper and intermediate layers. Outside the Atlantic surface layer, however, observed DIC accumulated systematically faster than TRACE, by 0.10–0.17 µmol kg⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (ratios 1.13–1.25). This excess was robust to 11 analysis variants and to leave-one-cruise-out tests. A separate DIC-based estimate, eMLR(C*) (built from the same GLODAP DIC with a different method), also exceeded TRACE by 10–35 % in these layers, while Southern Ocean intermediate waters showed the opposite sign. The excess lies below a conservative detection limit for cumulative measurement offsets, so it identifies a DIC-versus-tracer discrepancy, not a proven bias. The same signal confounds static empirical DIC regressions. A time covariate removed a median 77–83 % of their temporal-holdout bias, and their model rankings depended on the validation design.
